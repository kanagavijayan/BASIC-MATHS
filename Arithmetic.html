Arithmetic

Arithmetic is the foundation of mathematics, dealing with the four basic operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. These operations are the building blocks for more advanced topics like algebra, geometry, and calculus, and they are used daily in real-life tasks such as shopping, cooking, and budgeting.

🔢 Core Arithmetic Operations

  • Addition: Combining two or more numbers (e.g., 3+5=8).
  • Subtraction: Taking one number away from another (e.g., 9−4=5)
  • Multiplication: Repeated addition (e.g., 6×3=18).
  • Division: Splitting into equal parts (e.g., 20÷4=5).

⚙️ Properties of Arithmetic

  • Commutative: Order doesn’t matter (e.g., 3+7=7+3).
  • Associative: Grouping doesn’t change results (e.g., (2+3)+4=2+(3+4))
  • Distributive: Multiplication distributes over addition (e.g., 2×(3+4)=2×3+2×4).
  • Identity: Adding 0 or multiplying by 1 keeps the number unchanged

📊 Types of Arithmetic

  • Simple Arithmetic : Deals with basic operations — addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division — using whole numbers
  • Decimal Arithmetic : Focuses on numbers with decimal points, important for money, measurements, and precise calculations
  • Fractional Arithmetic : Works with fractions (parts of a whole), including addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of fractions.
  • Negative Arithmetic : Extends operations to negative numbers, useful in contexts like debts, temperatures, or below-zero values
  • Percentage Arithmetic : Involves ratios out of 100, widely used in finance, discounts, statistics, and growth rates
  • Integer Arithmetic : Focuses on whole numbers (positive, negative, and zero) without fractions or decimals.
  • Modulo Arithmetic : Deals with remainders after division, often used in computer science and cryptography.
